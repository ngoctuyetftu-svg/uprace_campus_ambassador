01 · The problem

The challenge, and what I proposed

The challenge

Students scrolled past everything the brand published.

  • Reach gap. Owned channels spoke to people who already knew the campaign — not the students it needed to convert.
  • Credibility gap. Running intimidates newcomers, and polished brand content made the barrier feel higher, not lower.
  • Campus gap. Four university events needed on-the-ground presence a central team could not create from outside.
The initiative

Put the message in the hands of students other students already follow.

  • New program, no precedent. The organisation had never run an ambassador layer — there was no playbook, roster or budget line to inherit.
  • Scouted, not advertised. Each candidate was sourced individually against a fit profile, then approached directly.
  • Their format, their voice. Ambassadors kept full creative control; the program supplied direction, not scripts.
02 · Execution

Scouting as a live pilot

The outreach doubled as a demand test. If students were not genuinely interested in running, the program would stall at recruitment — so the first invites were the experiment.

10/10

Every student approached accepted. The pilot answered the open question immediately: appetite for running activities among students was far higher than assumed. That signal justified scaling the program from a test into a full campaign track — and engagement stayed positive across every activity that followed.
